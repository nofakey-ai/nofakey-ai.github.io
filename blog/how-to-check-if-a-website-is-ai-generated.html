How to Check if a Website Is AI-Generated (and Whether to Trust It)

26 October 2026 9 min read Website safety

You land on a site with smooth copy, polished images and a product you want. Something feels slightly off, though. Was this built by a team, or spun up by AI in an afternoon? That question is common, but it is only half the story. AI is used by honest businesses and scammers alike, so "is it AI?" matters less than "is it trustworthy?" This guide covers both.

Quick answer You can spot clues that a site is AI-generated, such as generic text, no named people, repetitive pages and synthetic images, but none is proof, and scanners are only estimates. What matters more is who owns the site and whether it is a real business. Check the domain, contact details, policies, reviews and payment options before you trust it.
In this guide

    "AI-generated website" can mean three things

    MeaningWhat it isIs it a problem?
    AI-written contentText on the site was drafted by AINot by itself. Quality, accuracy and honesty matter
    AI-built siteDesign, layout or code created with AI site buildersNo. Many real businesses use them
    AI-made fake siteA shop, news site or brand imitation built quickly to deceiveYes. The deception is the problem, not the tool

    Keep that distinction in mind. A site made with AI can be legitimate, and a hand-built site can be a scam.

    Signs a site may be AI-generated

    These are prompts to look closer, not proof. Many human-run sites show some of them too.

    SignWhat it looks likeWhy it can mislead
    Generic, polished textSmooth wording that says little, with filler and buzzwordsPlenty of human marketing copy reads this way
    No named authors or teamNo bylines, no "about us" faces, no real people to findSmall businesses sometimes skip this
    Repetitive structureMany pages with the same layout and phrasing, differing only by a keyword or locationTemplates and programmatic pages exist on legitimate sites
    Impossible breadthOne site covering unrelated topics in "expert" tonesLarge publishers cover many topics
    Synthetic or stock imagesPeople or products with odd details, or the same faces reusedStock photos are common and harmless
    Errors and invented detailsWrong facts, broken links, fake citations or contradictions between pagesHuman sites have mistakes too
    No original materialNo original photos, data, reporting or customer examplesSome honest sites are simply thin
    Template look with generic brandingStandard layout, placeholder text left in, or a logo that looks auto-madeWebsite builders produce this for everyone

    How to check, step by step

    1. Read a few pages, not just the home page. Do product pages, blog posts and policies feel specific, or are they vague and interchangeable?
    2. Look for real people. Search the names of authors and team members. Do they exist outside the site?
    3. Check the images. Look for odd details, repeated faces and photos that appear on other sites. A reverse image search can show where a picture came from. See how journalists verify images.
    4. Compare writing across pages. Sudden shifts in tone, or many articles with the same pattern, can signal bulk production.
    5. Check facts and sources. Do the claims, quotes and statistics link to real sources that say what the site claims?
    6. Use a scanner, if you like, as a clue. Paste a sample of text into an AI detector to see the estimate, but remember the limits. See are AI detectors accurate?
    7. Move on to trust checks. Whether it is AI-made matters less than the checks in the next section.
    Simple rule: do not decide a site's trustworthiness from how "AI" its writing feels. Decide from who is behind it and how it treats your money and data.

    How to check if a website is trustworthy

    1. Look up the domain. Use a domain lookup service to see when it was registered and where. A shop that claims to be long-established but registered its domain very recently deserves caution. Ownership may be hidden for privacy reasons, so treat this as one clue.
    2. Check business details. Look for a real address, phone number and company name, then confirm them in an official business register for the country concerned.
    3. Test the contact route. Does the email use the site's own domain? Do the phone number and address make sense?
    4. Read the policies. Returns, shipping, privacy and terms should be specific. Copied or missing policies are a warning sign.
    5. Search for independent reviews. Look beyond the site's own testimonials, and be careful with reviews that look mass-produced. See how to detect AI-generated product reviews.
    6. Check how you found it. Ads, social posts and links in messages are common routes for fake shops. Type the known address yourself where possible.
    7. Check the address bar. A padlock only means the connection is encrypted. It does not mean the site is honest. Look for lookalike spellings of well-known brands.
    8. Look at payment options. Be wary of sites that accept only bank transfer, crypto or gift cards. Cards and well-known payment services usually offer more buyer protection.
    9. Check prices. Deals far below everywhere else, with countdown timers and limited stock, are a classic fake-shop pattern.

    Fake shops and AI: what changed

    AI makes it cheaper to create hundreds of convincing product pages, reviews and policy pages. A fake shop no longer needs obvious spelling mistakes. That is why the checks above focus on ownership, business identity and payments, not on how polished the writing is.     Content farms and AI news sites

    Some sites publish large volumes of AI-written articles, sometimes imitating local news. Signs include no named journalists, no original reporting, repeated structure and no corrections policy. If a story matters, check it with reliable sources. Our guide on AI-generated fake news checkers has a five-step check you can use.

    If you run a website

    • Add real people and experience. Named authors, credentials and first-hand examples build trust.
    • Be open about AI use where readers would expect it. Wording ideas are in how to label AI-generated content.
    • Check facts and sources before publishing, and fix errors quickly.
    • Publish clear business details, contact routes and policies.
    • Avoid mass-produced thin pages. See does Google penalize AI-generated content?
    • Audit regularly. Our guide to an AI content audit shows a framework you can adapt.

    If you think a site is fake

    1. Do not enter payment or personal details.
    2. If you already did, contact your bank or card provider straight away, and change any passwords you reused.
    3. Keep evidence: screenshots, order emails, website address and payment records.
    4. Report it to the platform where you found it, the web host if you can find it, and your national fraud reporting service or consumer authority.
    5. Warn others in a calm, factual way.

    Also be careful with browser extensions that claim to rate websites. Install them only from official stores and check their permissions. See the fake AI browser extension update scam for why.

    Conclusion

    You can sometimes tell a website was made with AI, but it rarely settles whether you should trust it. Look at the writing, images and structure for clues, then check ownership, business identity, policies, reviews and payment options. Treat scanners as hints, and never let polished design replace verification.     Frequently asked questions

    Can you tell if a website is AI-generated?

    Sometimes you can spot clues, such as generic text, no named authors, repetitive pages and stock or synthetic images, but none is proof. Many legitimate sites use AI in design or writing.

    Is an AI-generated website a scam?

    Not necessarily. AI is used by honest businesses and by scammers alike. Whether to trust a site depends on who is behind it, whether it is a real business and how it handles payments and contact, not on whether AI was involved.

    Are website AI scanners accurate?

    They estimate how much text resembles AI writing and can be wrong in both directions. Treat the result as a clue and check the site's ownership, contact details and reputation.

    How do I check if an online shop is real?

    Check how long the domain has existed and who owns it, look for a real address and business details, read independent reviews, check policies and payment options, and prefer payment methods that offer buyer protection.

    What should I do if I think a website is fake?

    Do not enter payment or personal details. If you already did, contact your bank, change passwords, keep evidence and report the site to the host, the platform where you found it and your national fraud reporting service.
