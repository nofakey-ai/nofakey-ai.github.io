How to Know If a Video Is AI Generated

2 November 2026 6 min read Deepfakes & video checks

A shocking clip lands in your feed. A famous face says something wild, or a “breaking news” scene looks almost too dramatic. Is it real? AI video tools have improved fast, so the old advice of “just look for glitches” only gets you part of the way. This short guide shows the signs worth checking, the habits that work better than staring at pixels, and the limits of any detector.

Quick answer Start with the source, then look for several small oddities, not one. Faces, hands, backgrounds, physics and voices can all give AI video away, but none of them is proof alone. If you cannot find the original upload or a trusted confirmation, treat the video as unverified.
In this guide

    Check the source before the pixels

    Most people squint at the video first. Try the opposite. Ask where it came from and who benefits if you believe it.

    • Who posted it first? A brand-new or anonymous account with no history is a warning sign. A repost with no credit is too.
    • Is anyone else showing it? A real event usually has more than one angle or report. One lonely clip is a reason to wait.
    • Does it push a strong emotion? Outrage, fear and “share before it’s deleted” are classic hooks, for fake news and scams alike.

    9 signs a video may be AI generated

    Think of these as clues. One odd moment can come from a bad camera. Several together are worth taking seriously.

    Visual signs

    What to checkWhat it can look like
    1. Faces and skinSkin that is too smooth, eyes that blink oddly or not at all, teeth that blur or change, expressions that don’t match the mood
    2. Hands and fingersExtra, fused or bending fingers, objects that melt into a hand
    3. BackgroundsWalls, crowds or signs that warp, shimmer or change between frames
    4. Text and logosGarbled letters on signs, shirts or screens; logos that look slightly off
    5. Physics and motionObjects that pass through each other, liquids or cloth that move strangely, floaty or “too smooth” camera moves
    6. Light and shadowsShadows pointing different ways, reflections that don’t match the scene

    Audio and context signs

    • 7. Lip sync. Mouth shapes that drift from the words, especially on fast speech or when the head turns.
    • 8. Voice. Flat tone, odd pacing, missing breaths, or room sound that doesn’t match the scene.
    • 9. Short, polished clips. Many AI clips are brief and cleanly cut. A sudden cut right where detail would matter is worth noticing.

    Remember that these tells fade as tools improve. A clip with none of them can still be fake.

    How to verify a video in a few minutes

    1. Find the original. Look at the earliest upload, the full video and the poster’s other content.
    2. Search the claim, not just the clip. Describe what it shows in a few words and see whether reliable outlets report the same event.
    3. Grab a few still frames. Screenshot a clear moment and run it through a reverse image search to spot older or reused footage.
    4. Ask the people shown. If a public figure is “caught” saying something, check their official channels for a response.
    5. Look for labels, not proof. Some platforms and tools mark AI content, but labels can be missing or removed.
    6. Slow down. Watch at half speed and with the sound off, then on. Odd details show up faster.
    7. Don’t share until you know. “Unverified” is a fine answer.

    What AI video detectors can’t do

    Free detectors are tempting, but treat their results as a hint, not a verdict.

    • They give odds, not proof. A score is an estimate and can be wrong either way.
    • New tools slip through. A detector trained on older fakes may miss newer ones.
    • Compression confuses them. Re-uploads and screen recordings strip detail, which can cause false alarms or hide real signs.
    • Real video can look fake. Filters, low light and beauty effects create glitches in genuine footage. Real videos are also sometimes dismissed as “AI” just because people don’t want to believe them.
    • Missing watermark means nothing. Marks can be cropped out or never added.

    When a fake video is part of a scam

    AI video is not only about misinformation. Scammers use fake endorsements, “urgent” messages from a boss or relative, and cloned faces on video calls to get you to send money or share codes. Whatever the video looks like, never act on money or login requests from it alone. Confirm by calling a number you already trust.     Frequently asked questions

    How can I tell if a video is AI generated?

    Check where it came from first, then look for visual and audio oddities: unnatural faces and hands, shifting backgrounds, impossible physics, mismatched lip sync and flat or robotic voices. No single sign is proof, so look for several together and confirm with trusted sources.

    Can a free AI video detector tell me for sure?

    No. Detectors give a probability, not proof. They can miss new AI tools and can wrongly flag real footage, especially after compression. Use them as one clue alongside source checks.

    Can a real video look AI generated?

    Yes. Heavy compression, filters, low light, beauty effects and screen recordings can make genuine footage look odd. That is why context and the original source matter more than visual glitches alone.

    Is a video with no watermark real?

    Not necessarily. Watermarks and labels can be cropped, removed or never added. A missing label proves nothing, and a visible label is only a hint about how it was made.

    What should I do before sharing a suspicious video?

    Pause, look for the original upload, see whether reliable outlets or the people shown have confirmed it, and avoid sharing if you cannot verify it. If it asks you to send money or details, treat it as a possible scam.

    Conclusion

    You don’t need to be a video expert. Check the source, look for a cluster of small oddities, confirm with trusted channels, and be honest when the answer is “can’t tell yet.” That habit protects you far better than hunting for one perfect giveaway. Find more guides on the Nofakey.ai blog.
