Fake UPI Refund Confirmation Scam: How It Works and How to Stay Safe

9 October 2026 8 min read UPI & money safety

Your phone buzzes. "Refund of ₹4,999 initiated. Tap to receive." Or a caller says a refund is stuck and only needs your "confirmation." It feels like good news, so you act fast. That is exactly what the fake UPI refund confirmation scam depends on.

Quick answer In a fake UPI refund scam, a message, call, QR code, or "confirmation" screenshot tricks you into approving a payment while you think you are receiving money. You never need to enter your UPI PIN, scan a QR code, or approve anything to receive money. Entering your PIN is only for sending money out.
In this guide

    How the fake UPI refund scam works

    UPI has two kinds of screens that look alike: one where you pay and one where someone requests money from you. Scammers blur the two, then use the word "refund" to lower your guard. Most versions follow four steps.

    1. The hook. A call, SMS, WhatsApp message, or social media reply says you are due a refund, cashback, or reversal.
    2. The "confirmation." You receive a request, a QR code, or a screenshot that looks like a bank or UPI app message.
    3. The push. You are told to approve, scan, or enter your PIN "to confirm" the refund, and to do it quickly.
    4. The debit. The action you take sends money out of your account, or hands over your PIN, OTP, or phone access.

    The missing piece: a "refund confirmation" is not proof

    Many guides tell you not to enter your PIN. Fewer explain how to check whether a refund really arrived. A screenshot, an SMS with a link, or a caller saying "it is done" proves nothing, because all of these can be faked. The only proof is your own bank or UPI app showing the credit in your transaction history or balance.

    A genuine refund also needs no action from you. It goes back to the account or card you paid from, and the company or bank will not need your PIN, OTP, or a QR scan to send it.

    Warning signs of a fake UPI refund

    • It asks for your UPI PIN or says "enter PIN to receive."
    • It asks you to scan a QR code or tap a request to "claim" money.
    • It asks for an OTP, card details, or CVV for "verification."
    • It asks you to install a screen-sharing or remote-access app.
    • It creates urgency: "refund will expire," "account will be blocked."
    • The amount is round or matches a recent payment, which makes the request feel believable.
    • The sender is unknown, or the number came from a search result, not the company's official site.
    • A "wrong payment" story follows: "I sent too much by mistake, please send it back."
    • The link or page looks off, with a strange address, odd wording, or spelling mistakes.

    Realistic examples

    These are composite examples written to show the pattern. They are not reports about real people, and the amounts are for illustration.

    Example 1: The failed order refund A buyer's online order fails. They search for "customer care" and call a number from the results. The agent says the refund of ₹2,499 needs "UPI confirmation," then sends a collect request. Approving it with the PIN debits ₹2,499 instead of crediting it.
    Example 2: The QR code "refund" A seller who has listed an item online gets a message from a "buyer" who says they will pay and refund any delivery difference. The buyer sends a QR code and says "scan to receive your money." The scan opens a payment screen, and entering the PIN sends money to the buyer.
    Example 3: The overpayment and fake screenshot Someone sends a screenshot showing ₹10,000 paid to you, then calls to say it was sent by mistake and asks you to return it. Your bank app shows no credit at all. Anything you send back is real money out of your pocket.
    Example 4: The remote-access "fix" A caller says a refund is stuck and asks you to install a screen-sharing app to "help." Once connected, they can see your screen, including OTPs and payment apps.

    Prevention tips

    • Remember one rule: enter your UPI PIN only when you are paying.
    • Decline collect requests from people or handles you do not know, even if the note says "refund."
    • Check refunds in your own bank or UPI app, not in screenshots or links.
    • Find customer care numbers on the company's official website or app, not in search snippets or AI summaries.
    • Never install a screen-sharing or remote-access app because a caller asked.
    • Never share an OTP, PIN, or CVV with anyone, including someone who says they are from your bank.
    • Set a sensible daily UPI limit and turn on transaction alerts.
    • Do not let anyone use your account or UPI ID to receive or move money for them.
    • Talk to parents and elders about this scam, since they are often targeted by "refund" calls.
    Simple rule: if the word "refund" comes with a PIN, a scan, an OTP, or an app install, it is a scam. A real refund asks nothing of you.

    What to do if you have been scammed (India)

    Speed matters. Do these steps in order and keep notes as you go.

    1. Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 right away. This is the quickest route for reporting online financial fraud in India.
    2. File a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in, the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. Choose the financial fraud category and note your acknowledgement number.
    3. Call your bank's fraud or customer care line using the number on your card or the bank's official website. Ask them to block the UPI ID, card, or account and to flag the transaction.
    4. Raise a dispute inside your UPI app. Open the transaction and use the complaint option. NPCI's process starts with the UPI app, then escalates to the bank and NPCI if unresolved. See the NPCI UPI dispute redressal page.
    5. If you installed a remote-access app, uninstall it, disconnect from the internet, change your UPI PIN and banking passwords from a safe device, and check for other apps you do not recognise.
    6. Save evidence: the transaction ID, UPI handle, phone numbers, chat and call screenshots, and any links.
    7. If the bank or app does not resolve it, write to the bank's grievance officer, and escalate to the RBI Ombudsman through the RBI complaint portal.

    No one can promise that the money will come back. Fast reporting gives the best chance that a transfer can be stopped or traced. Check the official sites above for current procedures, because they can change.

    Conclusion

    The fake UPI refund confirmation scam works because refunds sound like good news. The fix is simple and does not depend on spotting every trick: receiving money never needs your PIN, a scan, or an approval. When in doubt, close the message, open your own bank app, and see what is actually there.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need to enter my UPI PIN to receive a refund?

    No. Your UPI PIN is only for sending money. Money sent to you arrives without a PIN, a QR scan, or any approval. If someone asks you to enter your PIN to receive a refund, the action will take money out of your account.

    What is a fake UPI refund confirmation?

    It is a fake message, screenshot, or payment request that claims a refund has been sent or is waiting for you. Its purpose is to make you approve a collect request, scan a QR code, share an OTP or PIN, or send money back.

    How can I check if a refund really reached me?

    Open your own bank or UPI app and check your balance and transaction history. Do not trust a screenshot, an SMS link, or a caller's word. A genuine credit appears in your account without any action from you.

    What should I do right after losing money to a UPI refund scam?

    Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 immediately, file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in, and inform your bank to block the UPI ID, card, or account. Then raise a dispute on the transaction in your UPI app.

    Can I get my money back after a UPI scam?

    It depends on how quickly it is reported and where the money has moved, and nobody can promise recovery. Reporting at once to 1930, your bank, and your UPI app gives the best chance of the transfer being stopped or traced.
